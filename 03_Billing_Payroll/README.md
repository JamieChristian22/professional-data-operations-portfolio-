## 📖 Project Overview

This project demonstrates structured billing operations, accounts receivable reconciliation, payroll data processing, and financial quality-control procedures using Microsoft Excel, CSV datasets, and professional business documentation.

The project simulates a back-office operations team responsible for reviewing customer invoices, verifying payments, applying approved credits, preparing receivables reports, and reviewing employee gross payroll calculations.

The portfolio includes an original billing and payroll exercise along with an expanded 24-invoice accounts receivable reconciliation demonstration.

### 🎯 Business Objectives

- Validate customer invoice information and financial records.
- Verify invoice calculations and supporting service details.
- Reconcile customer payments and authorized credits.
- Track outstanding accounts receivable.
- Prepare financial reconciliation reports.
- Review employee timecards and pay rates.
- Calculate regular wages and overtime earnings.
- Identify unsupported transactions and payroll exceptions.
- Maintain accurate audit trails and approval controls.
- Prepare professionally documented financial outputs.

> **Portfolio disclosure:** All companies, employees, invoices, customers, and financial transactions are fictional. This project demonstrates simulated business operations rather than previous paid accounting, billing, or payroll employment.

# 💼 Business Scenario

## 🏢 Back-Office Billing & Payroll Close

A fictional service organization needs to complete its billing reconciliation and payroll review before an internal reporting cutoff.

The operations team must account for customer invoices, payments, authorized credits, employee hours, and pay rates.

The project is divided into two connected operational areas.

### 🧾 Area A — Billing & Accounts Receivable

The billing workflow includes:

1. Reviewing source invoice records.
2. Validating service quantities and amounts.
3. Matching posted customer payments.
4. Applying approved credit adjustments.
5. Calculating outstanding invoice balances.
6. Preparing accounts receivable reporting.
7. Recording exceptions and reconciliation evidence.

### 👥 Area B — Payroll Data Processing

The payroll workflow includes:

1. Reviewing approved employee timecards.
2. Verifying source pay rates.
3. Identifying regular and overtime hours.
4. Calculating gross earnings.
5. Reviewing unsupported or unapproved timecards.
6. Preparing verified gross payroll records.
7. Documenting approval requirements.

**Important:** This payroll demonstration excludes taxes, benefits, deductions, net pay, payment instructions, and production payroll release.

# 🧾 Module 01 — Invoice Processing & Validation

## 🎯 Objective

Review fictional billing records and confirm that invoice calculations are mathematically supported by documented service information.

### 🔍 Verification Procedures

- Confirm invoice identifiers.
- Review customer account references.
- Validate service quantities.
- Check listed unit prices.
- Recalculate invoice totals.
- Identify duplicate or inconsistent invoice information.
- Document records requiring additional review.

### 🧮 Basic Invoice Calculation

**Invoice Line Amount = Quantity × Unit Price**

**Invoice Gross = Sum of Invoice Line Amounts**

Any differences between source invoice details and calculated totals should be investigated before approval.

# 💳 Module 02 — Payments, Credits & Accounts Receivable

## 🎯 Objective

Accurately reconcile invoice balances using recorded payments and properly authorized credit adjustments.

### 🔧 Processing Steps

1. Identify valid invoice references.
2. Review posted payment records.
3. Verify payment amounts.
4. Match receipts to applicable invoices.
5. Review approved credits.
6. Calculate remaining receivable balances.
7. Investigate unmatched or misapplied transactions.
8. Prepare final reconciliation evidence.

### 🧮 Accounts Receivable Calculation

**Open Invoice Balance = Invoice Gross − Applied Payments − Approved Credits**

**Total Accounts Receivable = Sum of Open Invoice Balances**

### 📌 Key Controls

- Do not post unverified payments.
- Do not apply unsupported credits.
- Preserve source transaction references.
- Record mismatched payments as exceptions.
- Maintain a traceable relationship between source transactions and final balances.

# 👥 Module 04 — Payroll Data Entry & Verification

## 🎯 Objective

Review fictional employee time records and calculate gross earnings using documented hours, pay rates, and overtime assumptions.

### 🔎 Verification Procedures

- Confirm employee identifiers.
- Review approved timecards.
- Verify recorded hours.
- Check hourly pay rates.
- Identify overtime hours.
- Calculate gross earnings.
- Flag unsupported or unapproved records.
- Preserve review and approval evidence.

### ⏱️ Payroll Calculation Method

For the simplified hourly-pay example:

**Regular Pay = Regular Hours × Hourly Rate**

**Overtime Pay = Overtime Hours × Hourly Rate × 1.5**

**Gross Pay = Regular Pay + Overtime Pay**

Overtime is demonstrated for hours exceeding 40 in one fixed seven-day workweek using a simplified federal overtime scenario.

# ⚠️ Module 05 — Exception Management

## 🎯 Objective

Identify unsupported financial transactions and payroll records before they affect approved reporting outputs.

### 🔎 Exception Categories

| Exception | Required Control |
|---|---|
| 💳 Misapplied payment | Investigate and prevent unsupported posting |
| ⏱️ Unapproved timecard | Reject or hold pending authorization |
| 🧾 Invalid invoice information | Verify against source documentation |
| 🏷️ Unsupported credit | Require documented approval |
| 🔢 Calculation discrepancy | Recalculate and reconcile |
| 📋 Missing supporting evidence | Escalate for clarification |

### 📁 Exception Evidence

[⚠️ Misapplied Payment — Not Posted](./EXCEPTION_misapplied_payment_not_posted.csv)

[🚫 Rejected Unapproved Timecard](./EXCEPTION_rejected_unapproved_timecard.csv)

### 🛡️ Financial Control Principles

1. Never silently modify unsupported transactions.
2. Do not treat anticipated payments as posted receipts.
3. Preserve rejected or held records for review.
4. Record why an exception was identified.
5. Require authorization before posting adjustments.
6. Keep documented evidence of reconciliation decisions.
